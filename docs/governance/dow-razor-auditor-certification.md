## Purpose

The DoW Razor Auditor rubric provides an experimental structure for evaluating whether a declared system appears to:

- operate within a defined Recursion Stability Envelope;
- reduce or control Joules per Coherent Transition;
- preserve reusable compressed structure;
- reduce avoidable recomputation;
- identify possible Boundary Avoidance;
- document infrastructure and economic assumptions.

The rubric may support:

- internal research evaluation;
- architecture comparison;
- governance review;
- benchmark design;
- audit preparation;
- diagnostic reporting.

It does not itself provide:

- legal certification
- regulatory certification
- safety certification
- sovereign certification
- compliance approval
- infrastructure-deployment approval
- procurement eligibility
- independent validation
- production-readiness authorization

## Core Evaluation Principle

The rubric examines the proposition:

```text
A system should evaluate and reduce avoidable recursive burden
before relying on additional infrastructure expansion.
```

This is an evaluation principle.

It does not mean that every infrastructure expansion is inefficient, non-compliant, or an example of Boundary Avoidance.

## Section 1 — Draft Scoring Model

The historical rubric assigns five dimensions:

| Dimension | Draft weight |
|---|---:|
| Energetic Efficiency or JCT | 25% |
| Memory Preservation and Reuse | 20% |
| Recomputation Burden | 20% |
| Infrastructure Dependency | 20% |
| Economic Sustainability | 15% |

The draft weights total 100 percent.

Each dimension uses a provisional `0–4` scale:

| Score | Draft interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Severe deficiency under the declared rubric |
| 1 | Weak evidence of alignment |
| 2 | Mixed or transitional evidence |
| 3 | Strong bounded evidence |
| 4 | Strongest evidence within the declared evaluation |

These scores have no universal meaning outside the rubric’s documented scope.

## Section 2 — Measurement Requirements

Before scoring, the evaluator must define:

- evaluated system
- system version
- system boundary
- workload
- baseline
- operating scale
- time period
- hardware
- runtime
- resource budget
- measurement method
- evidence source
- uncertainty
- failure conditions
- evaluator identity
- conflicts of interest

A score must not be assigned from general impressions alone.

---

## Section 2.1 — Energetic Efficiency

The energetic-efficiency dimension may examine changes in JCT or another declared physical-energy measure.

A valid JCT evaluation must define:

- coherent transition
- energy boundary
- units
- measurement method
- time interval
- workload
- baseline
- uncertainty

Draft scoring orientation:

- materially decreasing measured JCT within preserved-quality bounds may support a higher score;
- no material change may support an intermediate score;
- materially increasing measured JCT may support a lower score.

An increase in JCT is not an automatic failure. It may reflect increased task quality, safety controls, changed workload, measurement error, or another justified tradeoff.

---

## Section 2.2 — Memory Preservation and Reuse

This dimension may examine whether the system preserves and reuses required:

- identity
- relationships
- provenance
- constraints
- version state
- retrieval paths
- evidence status
- output fidelity

Draft scoring orientation:

- strong measured preservation and reuse may support a higher score;
- partial preservation or mixed evidence may support an intermediate score;
- repeated loss and reconstruction of required structure may support a lower score.

Storage volume alone does not establish memory quality.

---

## Section 2.3 — Recomputation Burden

This dimension may examine:

- repeated derivation
- repeated retrieval
- token expansion
- repeated verification
- duplicated processing
- correction demand
- reconstruction cost

Draft scoring orientation:

- materially reduced avoidable recomputation may support a higher score;
- stable or mixed results may support an intermediate score;
- increasing avoidable recomputation may support a lower score.

The evaluator must distinguish necessary computation from avoidable recomputation.

---

## Section 2.4 — Infrastructure Dependency

This dimension may examine how the system’s declared performance depends on:

- compute scale
- energy
- memory
- network capacity
- cooling
- storage
- external services
- organizational coordination
- vendor-specific infrastructure

Draft scoring orientation:

- measured resilience within fixed constraints may support a higher score;
- partial dependence may support an intermediate score;
- continual expansion without measured internal improvement may support a lower score.

Infrastructure dependence is not inherently non-compliant.

The evaluation must consider the complete system purpose, scale, reliability requirements, and alternatives.

---

## Section 2.5 — Economic Sustainability

This dimension may compare declared value or utility against:

- capital cost
- operating cost
- energy cost
- maintenance
- labor
- licensing
- infrastructure
- governance
- correction
- replacement
- risk

Draft scoring orientation:

- documented value exceeding declared total cost may support a higher score;
- marginal or uncertain results may support an intermediate score;
- documented cost exceeding value under the declared assumptions may support a lower score.

Economic scoring requires an explicit time horizon, accounting method, assumptions, uncertainty, and responsible analyst.

## Section 3 — Draft Composite Score

The historical weighted model is:

```text
composite_score
=
Σ(dimension_score × dimension_weight)
```

With dimensions scored from `0` to `4` and weights totaling `1.0`, the composite remains on a provisional `0–4` scale.

Historical assessment bands are preserved as:

| Composite score | Draft assessment band |
|---:|---|
| 3.20–4.00 | Razor-Aligned within declared scope |
| 2.00–3.19 | Transitional or mixed |
| 0.00–1.99 | High brute-force or instability concern |

These are experimental assessment bands.

They are not certification levels, regulatory findings, or deployment decisions.

---

## Section 4 — Review Triggers

The historical rubric described several conditions as immediate failures.

Under MRD v2.0 evidence governance, they should instead be treated as **mandatory review triggers**:

- materially increasing JCT;
- materially increasing avoidable recomputation;
- dependence on continuing external subsidy;
- crossing a declared collapse boundary;
- loss of required provenance;
- loss of required reusable structure;
- correction demand exceeding stabilization capacity;
- recursive blast radius exceeding the declared limit.

A review trigger requires investigation.

It does not determine the final result without context, evidence, alternatives, uncertainty, and declared thresholds.

---

## Section 5 — Draft Assessment Bands

### Band A — Razor-Aligned Within Declared Scope

Possible characteristics:

- bounded stability under tested constraints;
- measured improvement in declared transition cost;
- strong preserved reuse;
- controlled recomputation;
- explicit evidence and failure boundaries.

### Band B — Transitional or Mixed

Possible characteristics:

- partial compression or reuse;
- mixed performance;
- incomplete evidence;
- improving but unstable behavior;
- unresolved review triggers.

### Band C — High Concern

Possible characteristics:

- unbounded or unexplained scaling;
- repeated loss of required structure;
- increasing avoidable recomputation;
- instability under declared constraints;
- insufficient evidence for alignment.

These bands are descriptive outputs from a draft rubric.

They must not be labeled `certified`, `restricted`, `non-compliant`, or `approved` without an authorized external program defining those terms.

## Section 6 — Deployment Boundary

This rubric does not determine whether a system is:

- eligible for critical-infrastructure deployment;
- restricted;
- subject to monitoring;
- prohibited;
- suitable for sovereign use;
- safe for public deployment;
- compliant with law or regulation.

Those determinations require the applicable legal authority, technical standards, security review, safety review, procurement process, and responsible decision-makers.

A rubric score may be one input to a broader review only when the receiving organization explicitly authorizes that use.

## Boundary Avoidance Requirement

Infrastructure scaling should be labeled Boundary Avoidance only when the evaluation demonstrates that:

1. a relevant internal recursion cost or inefficiency exists;
2. infrastructure expansion displaces or masks that burden;
3. the expansion does not produce a sufficient measured internal improvement;
4. alternatives and counterevidence were considered.

Infrastructure growth alone does not establish non-alignment.
