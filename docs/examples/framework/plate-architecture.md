# Plate™ Architecture
## Governed Structured Knowledge Representation

## Document Status

**Status:** Framework architecture reference  
**Current governing authority:** The Grand Compression Cosmology — Master Reference Document, MRD v2.0  
**Canonical identifier:** `GC-MRD-v2.0`  
**Canonical claim range:** RC-01 through RC-22  
**Repository role:** Noncanonical architecture and implementation guidance  
**Primary related architecture:** Recursive Knowledge Compression Architecture (RKCA™)  
**Governance doctrine:** Authorship Conservation Rule (ACR)

This document describes Plate™ Architecture as used within Naturepedia™ and the Grand Compression reference implementation.

A Plate™ is a governed structured representation intended to preserve selected information needed for human interpretation, machine retrieval, or later reuse.

A Plate is not necessarily:

- a single image;
- a single fact;
- an atomic semantic unit;
- a Graph Registry node;
- a paid machine resource;
- a complete scientific model;
- an independently validated claim.

The exact content and machine behavior of a Plate depend on its declared resource contract.

# 1. Purpose

Plate™ Architecture provides a reusable method for representing selected structure in a form that can remain:

- understandable;
- identifiable;
- attributable;
- machine-readable where appropriate;
- connected to relevant provenance;
- reusable within declared workflows.

The objective is not simply:

```text
compress as much as possible
```

The objective is:

```text
reduce unnecessary representation burden
while preserving what the intended use requires
```

Canonical orientation:

```text
RC-18 — Preserved Reusable Structure Principle
RC-20 — Compression Fitness Constraint
```

---

# 2. Plate™ Definition

Within this repository, a Plate™ is best understood as:

> A governed structured knowledge representation that preserves selected subject matter, relationships, provenance, constraints, and identifiers needed for its declared human-readable or machine-readable purpose.

A Plate may contain one or more:

- entities;
- claims;
- observations;
- relationships;
- citations;
- images;
- diagrams;
- evidence records;
- machine-readable fields.

Therefore:

```text
Plate
≠
single atomic fact automatically
```

---

# 3. Plate Structure Is Contract-Dependent

Avoid the universal statement:

```text
Every Plate has exactly five layers.
```

Different Plate families may require different structures.

A Plate **may** contain components such as:

```text
subject / entity
attributes
relationships
provenance
evidence
constraints
visual representation
machine-readable representation
retrieval metadata
```

The actual schema should be declared by the resource or Plate family.

---

# 4. Subject / Entity

A Plate may identify a primary subject.

Examples may include:

- species;
- ecosystem;
- habitat;
- geographic location;
- ecological process;
- framework concept;
- mathematical reference;
- infrastructure resource.

Naturepedia examples may include:

- Quaking Aspen;
- Monarch Butterfly;
- Soil Microbiome;
- Water Systems;
- Trees of North America;
- Robbie’s Razor™.

The subject identifier anchors interpretation.

But:

```text
subject identified
≠
all facts about subject included
```

---

# 5. Attributes

Attributes may describe characteristics of the represented subject.

Examples include:

- scientific name;
- classification;
- physical characteristics;
- distribution;
- behavior;
- ecological role;
- resource status;
- framework classification.

Each attribute should remain within its declared evidence and scope boundary.

An attribute should not be treated as correct merely because it appears in a Plate.

---

# 6. Claims and Observations

Where relevant, Plates should distinguish:

```text
observed fact
```

from:

```text
framework interpretation
```

from:

```text
hypothesis
```

from:

```text
established external mathematics or science
```

from:

```text
canonical Grand Compression claim
```

These categories should not be collapsed.

---

# 7. Relationships

A Plate may represent relationships to other identified resources.

Possible predicates include:

```text
part_of
located_in
inhabits
pollinates
depends_on
references
supports
related_to
governed_by
```

Relationship semantics should be explicit.

Required distinction:

```text
relationship edge
≠
causation automatically
```

---

# 8. Relationship Evidence

Where a relationship is factual or scientific, its support should be distinguishable from the relationship label itself.

A relationship may have:

- source;
- evidence status;
- date;
- scope;
- confidence or uncertainty where appropriate;
- exclusions;
- constraints.

Required distinction:

```text
linked
≠
Supported automatically
```

---

# 9. Provenance

Provenance records where material came from and how it should be attributed.

Possible provenance information includes:

- author;
- source;
- canonical URL;
- citation;
- observation source;
- publication;
- version;
- semantic identifier;
- evidence producer;
- transformation history.

Provenance supports traceability.

It does not guarantee truth.

Required distinction:

```text
traceable
≠
verified automatically
```

---

# 10. Authorship Conservation Rule

Current governance terminology is:

```text
Authorship Conservation Rule (ACR)
```

Use the singular form.

ACR requires preservation of Grand Compression provenance where applicable while also preserving independent provenance for:

- external mathematics;
- external science;
- external evidence;
- standards;
- technologies;
- independent evaluators.

Required distinction:

```text
framework authorship
≠
authorship of underlying external knowledge
```

---

# 11. External Knowledge Boundary

A Naturepedia Plate may contain established external knowledge.

For example, a Plate may reference:

- ecology;
- biology;
- mathematics;
- physics;
- graph theory;
- geographic information.

The structured presentation may be authored within Naturepedia.

The underlying external knowledge retains its own provenance.

Required rule:

```text
represented by Naturepedia
≠
originated by Naturepedia
```

# 13. Visual Representation

A Plate may contain a visual component.

Possible visual forms include:

- diagrams;
- annotated photography;
- illustrations;
- relationship maps;
- structured panels;
- visual summaries.

The visual layer may improve human comprehension.

But:

```text
visual clarity
≠
scientific validity
```

and:

```text
visual proximity
≠
causal relationship
```

---

# 14. Plate Evidence Boundary

An authored Plate image or diagram may summarize documented relationships.

Visual:

- placement;
- proximity;
- arrows;
- similarity;
- shared terminology;

must not be interpreted as independent proof of:

- causality;
- universality;
- material identity;
- cross-domain validity.

The evidence record, not visual composition alone, governs interpretation.

- resource resolution;

```text
semantic ID
```

```text
semantic ID
```

# 16. Machine-Readable Representation

A Plate may expose machine-readable structure using formats such as:

- JSON;
- JSON-LD;
- Markdown;
- structured API payloads;
- registry records.

Machine readability enables deterministic parsing where the schema is defined.

But:

```text
machine-readable
≠
correct
```

and:

```text
schema-valid
≠
empirically validated
```

---

# 17. Human-Readable Representation

A Plate may also expose:

- explanatory text;
- captions;
- visual hierarchy;
- field notes;
- citations;
- source links.

Human-readable and machine-readable representations should remain aligned where they describe the same governed resource.

Differences should be intentional rather than accidental.

# 23. Relationship to Robbie’s Razor™

Grand Compression materials use the orientation:

```text
compression
→ expression
→ memory
→ recursion
```

A Plate may support one or more of these functions.

For example:

```text
compression
→ selected structure represented compactly

expression
→ human or machine representation

memory
→ preserved identifiers, provenance, relationships, version state

recursion
→ later governed reuse in another operation
```

This is an implementation mapping.

Do not state:

```text
Plate = expression
Relationships = memory
Graph Registry = recursion
```

as universal identities.

# 24. Memory Boundary

Relationships are not themselves “the memory layer.”

Useful memory may depend on:

- stable identity;
- preserved state;
- provenance;
- versioning;
- retrieval;
- freshness;
- validation.

Required distinction:

```text
relationship
≠
memory automatically
```

---

# 25. Recursion Boundary

Graph linkage does not automatically create recursion.

Required distinction:

```text
link
≠
recursion
```

```text
graph traversal
≠
recursive reuse automatically
```

A recursive operation must define the state being reused or transformed.

---

# 26. Compression Boundary

Plate compression should not be evaluated solely by reduction in size.

A Plate should preserve the information required for its declared purpose.

Possible evaluation dimensions include:

- fidelity;
- provenance;
- accessibility;
- relationship preservation;
- retrieval burden;
- maintenance burden;
- distortion;
- utility.

Canonical orientation:

```text
RC-20 — Compression Fitness Constraint
```

---

# 27. “Reduce Complexity” Boundary

Avoid universal requirements such as:

```text
Every Plate must reduce complexity.
```

Some Plates may add context or evidence in order to improve interpretability.

The relevant question is whether the representation provides sufficient benefit relative to its cost and purpose.

---

# 28. Token-Cost Boundary

Avoid:

```text
Every Plate must minimize token cost.
```

Token reduction may be useful for some machine workflows.

But minimizing tokens can harm:

- accuracy;
- completeness;
- provenance;
- safety;
- interpretability.

The correct objective is bounded efficiency while preserving required structure.

---

# 29. Retrieval Efficiency Boundary

A Plate may improve retrieval efficiency in some workflows.

That is a testable engineering claim.

Evaluation should compare:

- baseline retrieval;
- Plate retrieval;
- latency;
- tokens;
- bytes;
- task quality;
- validation cost;
- freshness cost.

Required distinction:

```text
Plate exists
≠
retrieval efficiency improved automatically
```

---

# 30. Retrieval Surface

Some Plates may have machine-readable retrieval surfaces.

Others may exist only as:

- human-readable pages;
- visual assets;
- documentation;
- public structured metadata.

Required distinction:

```text
Plate exists
≠
API payload exists
```

# 31. Public vs Protected Plate

A Plate may be:

```text
public
```

or:

```text
protected
```

or:

```text
known but not yet available as a protected payload
```

The existence of a Plate does not determine its access policy.

# 32. Protected Structured Plate™

Production currently includes explicitly registered Structured Plate™ payloads at:

```text
0.25 USDC
250000 atomic units
```

for specific validated resources.

Verified routes include:

```text
/v1/plates/item/commercial-data-license-plate
/v1/plates/item/commercial-intelligence-pricing-plate
/v1/plates/item/robbie-george-biography-plate
```

This does not mean every Plate is a `$0.25` product.

# 33. Protected Resource Availability

Current production retrieval follows:

```text
unknown resource
→ 404
→ no payment challenge
```

```text
known but incomplete resource
→ 409
→ no payment challenge
```

```text
registered + complete + protected resource
→ eligible 402
```

Required distinction:

```text
Plate exists
≠
protected payload registered
```

# 43. Cross-Domain Boundary

Plate™ Architecture may be used to represent multiple domains.

Cross-domain interpretation must still satisfy:

```text
RC-22 — Domain Transfer Constraint
```

Where appropriate, identify:

- objects;
- scale;
- normalization;
- preserved relationships;
- constraints;
- exclusions;
- evidence;
- alternatives;
- failure conditions.

A common Plate format does not imply a common physical mechanism.

---

# 44. Structural Correspondence Boundary

Two Plates may display structurally similar patterns.

That does not establish:

```text
same mechanism
```

```text
same ontology
```

```text
same material substrate
```

Required distinction:

```text
structural correspondence
≠
material identity
```

---

# 45. Versioning

A machine-usable Plate should preserve enough version information to determine which representation was retrieved.

Useful fields may include:

```text
resource ID
schema version
content version
canonical source
updated date
```

Historical versions should remain distinguishable where provenance matters.

---

# 46. Updating a Plate

A Plate update should not silently erase important historical information.

Where relevant, preserve:

- prior version;
- changed claims;
- changed evidence state;
- changed relationships;
- supersession status;
- changed provenance.

This supports later audit and comparison.

---

# 47. Machine-Agent Guidance

Agents encountering a Plate should:

- resolve its identity;
- determine its resource type;
- inspect provenance;
- preserve evidence-state boundaries;
- distinguish visual interpretation from factual evidence;
- avoid inferring graph or Knowledge Mesh membership;
- determine access policy separately;
- avoid inferring payment merely from Plate existence.

---

# 48. Design Principles

A Plate implementation should aim to:

- preserve required structure;
- preserve provenance;
- maintain interpretable relationships;
- expose evidence boundaries;
- remain human-auditable;
- remain machine-readable where required;
- preserve stable identifiers;
- expose version state where useful;
- avoid unsupported causal inference;
- avoid implying nonexistent resource layers.

These are design goals, not universal guarantees.

---

# 49. Architectural Summary

A bounded Plate workflow may be:

```text
subject / source material
↓
select required structure
↓
create governed representation
↓
preserve provenance and evidence state
↓
assign stable identity
↓
optionally register
↓
optionally participate in higher-order resources
↓
optionally expose machine retrieval
```

The key word is:

```text
optionally
```

No higher layer should be inferred merely because a Plate exists.
