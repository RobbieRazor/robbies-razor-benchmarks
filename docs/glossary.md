It is an implementation aid and does not replace the complete canonical definitions, qualifications, boundaries, or evidence statements in **The Grand Compression Cosmology — Master Reference Document, MRD v2.0**.

## Grand Compression Cosmology

A framework developed by Robbie George for examining how systems compress, express, preserve, retrieve, and recursively reuse structure under physical, informational, computational, ecological, economic, and governance constraints.

Cross-domain application requires explicit declaration of objects, scale, normalization, relationships, exclusions, constraints, evidence, alternatives, and failure conditions.

---

## Robbie’s Razor™

A governing principle for evaluating whether a system preserves useful structure while reducing unnecessary energetic, computational, informational, organizational, or governance burden.

Robbie’s Razor does not mean that the smallest representation is automatically the best representation. Compression must remain accountable to fidelity, provenance, accessibility, constraints, and downstream utility.

---

## Compression

Reduction of system description, representation, or operating burden while preserving the structure necessary for the intended purpose.

Compression is the first stage of the canonical recursion cycle:

```text
compression → expression → memory → recursion
```

Canonical orientation: MRD §4.

---

## Expression

The transformation of compressed structure into an observable, operational, or retrievable state.

Expression may include physical behavior, computation, representation, communication, or structured publication.

Expression quality depends on whether the relevant constraints and preserved structure survive the transformation.

---

## Memory

Durable preservation of structure, relationships, provenance, constraints, or state required for later retrieval and reuse.

Memory is not limited to biological recall or digital storage. Its meaning depends on the declared domain, object, scale, and mechanism.

---

## Recursion

Repeated reuse or transformation of preserved structure across successive cycles or layers.

Stable recursion requires more than repetition. Identity, relationships, provenance, constraints, version state, and retrieval paths must remain sufficiently intact for the intended use.

Canonical orientation: MRD §11.

---

## Preserved Reusable Structure

Structure retained with enough identity, relationships, provenance, constraints, version state, and retrieval accessibility to support later use without uncontrolled reconstruction.

Preserved reusable structure is central to distinguishing durable compression from destructive simplification.

Canonical orientation: MRD v2.0, Section 13 and RC-18.

---

## Predictive Compression

A bounded form of compression evaluated partly by whether a reduced representation preserves enough relevant structure to support declared predictions or comparisons.

Predictive-compression claims must specify variables, scope, scale, baseline, direction, failure conditions, evidence status, and revision conditions.

Canonical orientation: MRD v2.0, Section 13 and RC-19.

---

## Compression Fitness

A provisional evaluation concept for comparing compression utility, fidelity, provenance, and accessibility against energetic, informational, governance, distortion, blast-radius, and maintenance costs.

The candidate Compression Fitness objective function remains **provisional** in Appendix Q. It must not be presented as a finalized universal law, validated metric, or canonical empirical result.

---

## Recursive Knowledge Compression Architecture (RKCA™)

An implementation architecture for converting complex knowledge into progressively reusable layers while preserving identity, relationships, provenance, constraints, and retrieval paths.

The general publication sequence is:

```text
Plate™ → Registry → System Map → Knowledge Mesh
```

Canonical orientation: MRD §12.6A.

---

## Recursive Compression Interface (RCI)

A bounded interface through which compressed knowledge can be retrieved, interpreted, connected, or expanded while preserving declared structural and governance constraints.

A Plate™ may function as a human-readable and machine-addressable Recursive Compression Interface.

Canonical orientation: MRD §12.6A.

---

## Plate™

A bounded knowledge object designed to compress a subject into an accessible visual and conceptual interface while connecting to deeper structured resources.

A Plate may include:

- stable identity
- human-readable explanation
- visual hierarchy
- canonical terminology
- provenance
- relationships
- machine-readable identifiers
- registry membership
- retrieval paths

A Plate is an implementation structure and does not independently validate every claim represented within it.

---

## Registry

A structured collection of identified knowledge objects and their governing metadata.

A registry may preserve:

- stable identifiers
- canonical paths
- object types
- version state
- provenance
- relationships
- evidence status
- protected retrieval paths

Registry membership does not by itself establish empirical validation.

---

## System Map

A structured representation of relationships among objects within a declared system boundary.

A System Map should identify its scope, scale, normalization choices, exclusions, constraints, and evidence status.

Visual or structural similarity between two systems does not by itself establish material identity, causal equivalence, or shared mechanism.

---

## Knowledge Mesh

A higher-order network connecting multiple Plates, registries, System Maps, relationships, constraints, and retrieval paths.

A Knowledge Mesh is intended to support cross-resource navigation and machine retrieval while preserving provenance and governance boundaries.

---

## Recursive Registry Inheritance Principle (RRIP)

A principle governing how identified objects, relationships, constraints, provenance, and version state are inherited across registry and knowledge layers.

Inheritance must preserve required invariants and must not silently introduce unsupported relationships or erase source distinctions.

Canonical orientation: MRD §12.6A.

---

## Canonical Serialization

A deterministic representation of structured data used so that equivalent governed content produces a stable serialized form for validation, hashing, settlement binding, or verified delivery.

Canonical serialization confirms representation consistency. It does not independently confirm the empirical truth of the serialized content.

---

## Canonical Authority

The designated source that resolves the current governing version, terminology, claims, and status of framework materials.

For this repository, the current governing authority is MRD v2.0, identified as `GC-MRD-v2.0`.

The repository implements and aligns with that authority but does not replace it.

---

## Canonical Claim

A numbered claim formally registered within the governing authority architecture.

The MRD v2.0 canonical claim range is RC-01 through RC-22.

Canonical claims must not be silently renumbered, paraphrased as exact quotations, or expanded beyond their stated boundaries.

---

## Authorship Conservation Rule (ACR)

A governance rule requiring preservation of original authorship, conceptual provenance, and attribution as framework concepts move through documents, implementations, agents, registries, and derived resources.

Implementation, summarization, or machine transformation does not transfer authorship of the originating framework.

---

## Evidence Status

A declaration describing the support level for a statement, model, comparison, implementation, or prediction.

Evidence status may distinguish among categories such as:

- canonical framework statement
- implementation example
- benchmark result
- independently reproduced result
- hypothesis
- proposal
- provisional construct
- analogy
- illustrative comparison

Canonical status, implementation status, endpoint availability, payment settlement, and successful serialization are not substitutes for empirical validation.

---

## Reference Implementation

A concrete implementation used to demonstrate how an architecture, schema, or governance contract may be expressed operationally.

Naturepedia™ is the primary reference implementation of the Grand Compression architecture.

Reference-implementation status demonstrates translation into a working structure; it does not establish universal validity or independent confirmation.

Canonical orientation: MRD v2.0 and RC-21.

---

## Joules per Coherent Transition (JCT)

An orientation metric for the energy cost associated with producing a coherent state transition that preserves the structure required for continued operation or reuse.

Any empirical JCT claim must identify its system boundary, measurement method, variables, units, baseline, and uncertainty.

Canonical orientation: MRD §11.4.5.

---

## Memory–Compute Stability Minimum

A proposed architectural balance between recomputation burden and preserved compressed memory under declared constraints.

Applications must define what is stored, what is recomputed, the relevant costs, the operating scale, and the failure conditions.

Canonical orientation: MRD §11.4.5A.

---

## Optionality Preservation Principle

The principle that recursive systems may improve resilience by preserving reachable future states rather than prematurely collapsing the available state space.

Its use in a specific domain requires declared variables, mechanisms, scale, constraints, evidence, and alternatives.

Canonical orientation: MRD §11.4.7A.

---

## Recursive Blast Radius Limit

A governance and architecture boundary describing how far a recursive modification may propagate before it creates unacceptable instability or unintended effects across connected layers.

Blast-radius limits should be explicit, testable, and appropriate to the system’s scale and risk.

Canonical orientation: MRD §11.11A.

---

## Boundary Avoidance

A failure mode in which a system expands external resource, infrastructure, or organizational boundaries instead of reducing internal recursion cost or correcting structural inefficiency.

A boundary-avoidance claim must identify the relevant system boundary, costs, alternatives, and observed failure pattern.

Canonical orientation: MRD §11.6A.

---

## Perishable Intelligence Asset (PIA)

An intelligence-producing system whose useful capacity may decay faster than assumed by its economic, computational, or organizational model.

Assessment requires declared time horizons, maintenance costs, replacement assumptions, evidence, and failure conditions.

Canonical orientation: MRD §11.6C.

---

## Governance Constraint

A rule that limits how a resource, claim, route, schema, or recursive operation may be created, transformed, retrieved, priced, or represented.

Governance constraints may include:

- authority requirements
- provenance requirements
- evidence labels
- version controls
- schema validation
- protected-resource routing
- settlement requirements
- blast-radius limits
- strict failure behavior

---

## Cross-Domain Transfer

Application of a concept, relationship, metric, or model from one domain or scale to another.

Cross-domain transfer is not justified by resemblance alone. It requires explicit objects, scale, normalization, relationships, exclusions, constraints, evidence, alternatives, and failure conditions.

Canonical orientation: MRD v2.0 and RC-22.

---

## Implementation Boundary

The distinction between demonstrating that a framework can be encoded or operated and demonstrating that its empirical claims are independently supported.

A working repository, Worker, endpoint, payment route, schema, Plate, registry, System Map, or Knowledge Mesh establishes implementation status within its declared scope. It does not automatically establish empirical validation.
