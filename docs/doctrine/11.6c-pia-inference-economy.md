# Perishable Intelligence Asset — Bounded Orientation

The Perishable Intelligence Asset model examines a possible mismatch between:

```text
observed useful-capacity durability
```

and:

```text
assumed useful-capacity durability
```

within a declared technical, organizational, governance, or economic model.

A PIA assessment may investigate whether useful structure is insufficiently preserved across changes involving:

- hardware;
- software;
- models;
- infrastructure;
- services;
- data;
- organizational knowledge;
- interfaces.

A changing system is not automatically a Perishable Intelligence Asset.

Required distinction:

```text
rapid change
≠
PIA
```

A bounded PIA assessment requires evidence.

---

# Intelligence-Persistence Boundary

Earlier wording in this file described intelligence as necessarily requiring fresh energetic regeneration unless compressed into durable memory.

That statement is too broad for repository-level empirical interpretation.

Different systems preserve useful capability through many mechanisms, including:

- model weights;
- external memory;
- databases;
- caches;
- structured state;
- software;
- policies;
- deterministic routines;
- human processes;
- retrieval systems.

The relevant empirical question is not whether intelligence is universally “perishable.”

The relevant question is:

> What useful capability must be reconstructed, what can be validly preserved, and what are the respective costs within the declared system?

Required distinction:

```text
fresh inference required
≠
all intelligence regenerated from zero
```

---

# Training and Inference Boundary

Training and inference can have different cost structures.

However, the simple statement:

```text
training is episodic
inference is continuous
```

must not be treated as universally true.

Real systems may involve:

- continuous training;
- periodic fine-tuning;
- online learning;
- batch inference;
- intermittent inference;
- retrieval;
- cached computation;
- deterministic processing.

Therefore:

```text
training
≠
always episodic
```

and:

```text
inference
≠
always continuous
```

The workload must be measured.

# Inference Economy — Bounded Orientation

The Inference Economy model examines systems in which deployed inference demand becomes a material operating burden.

Possible constraints may include:

- compute;
- energy;
- memory;
- networking;
- storage;
- latency;
- cooling;
- capital;
- governance;
- coordination.

Whether inference is the dominant constraint is an empirical question.

A system may instead be limited by:

- training;
- data;
- hardware supply;
- software;
- demand;
- networking;
- regulation;
- organizational capacity;
- another bottleneck.

Required distinction:

```text
large inference workload
≠
inference is the dominant system constraint
```

# Governance Boundary

Continuous or highly autonomous operation may create substantial governance demand.

Possible burdens include:

- validation;
- permission management;
- review;
- incident handling;
- correction;
- audit;
- rollback.

But increased inference does not automatically imply governance instability.

Required distinction:

```text
more inference
≠
governance saturation
```

and:

```text
governance demand exceeds capacity
≠
complete system failure
```

Governance-capacity relationships must be measured.

---

# Compression Is Not the Only Possible Response

Earlier wording in this file stated that compression was the **only durable relief** from PIA pressure.

That statement should not be retained.

A system may respond to workload or infrastructure pressure through combinations of:

- compression;
- caching;
- retrieval;
- better algorithms;
- specialized hardware;
- increased infrastructure;
- scheduling;
- workload prioritization;
- reduced demand;
- architectural redesign;
- improved networking;
- additional governance capacity.

The correct intervention depends on the evaluated system.

Required distinction:

```text
compression-first framework preference
≠
compression is the only technically valid intervention
```

# Economic Boundary

Technical observations do not automatically establish:

- profitability;
- competitiveness;
- asset value;
- capital efficiency;
- lower capex;
- lower opex;
- delayed replacement;
- investment superiority.

Therefore:

```text
technical efficiency
≠
economic competitiveness
```

and:

```text
preserved reusable structure
≠
long-run market advantage
```

unless an appropriate economic analysis supports the claim.

# Competitiveness Boundary

Earlier wording stated that long-run AI competitiveness is determined by how efficiently systems transform perishable intelligence into reusable structure.

That should instead be treated as a bounded strategic hypothesis.

Competitive outcomes may depend on:

- model capability;
- data;
- product quality;
- hardware;
- latency;
- reliability;
- safety;
- economics;
- distribution;
- regulation;
- user demand;
- organizational execution;
- reuse architecture.

No single repository concept should be presented as universally determining market competitiveness.
