# P.8 Priority Claims

A priority claim concerns when a specific formulation, term, architecture, or documented claim entered the record.

Priority should be supported through evidence such as:

- dated publications;
- version-controlled repositories;
- archived webpages;
- timestamped PDFs;
- public releases;
- signed records;
- persistent identifiers;
- other verifiable records.

A statement such as:

```text
Concept X was documented by Robbie George on date Y.
```

is stronger when the dated record can be independently inspected.

Required distinction:

```text
authorship assertion
≠
documented priority evidence
```

# P.11 Convergent Development

Independent researchers may arrive at similar ideas without accessing or deriving them from Grand Compression.

This can occur because different investigators encounter similar:

- optimization problems;
- computational limits;
- memory constraints;
- physical constraints;
- architectural tradeoffs.

The appropriate neutral term is:

```text
independent convergent development
```

when independence is supported or reasonably established.

Similarity alone does not determine whether the relationship is:

- independent convergence;
- common ancestry;
- citation;
- influence;
- derivation;
- coincidence.

---

# P.12 Convergent Rediscovery Boundary

The phrase:

```text
convergent rediscovery
```

should be used carefully.

It may be appropriate when an external work independently reaches a proposition that was previously documented within Grand Compression.

But that characterization requires:

- identification of the specific earlier proposition;
- dated evidence;
- identification of the later work;
- meaningful structural similarity;
- no unsupported implication of copying.

Do not label every adjacent external idea as a rediscovery of Grand Compression.

Required distinction:

```text
related idea
≠
rediscovery
```

---

# P.13 Derivation Boundary

A claim that an external work derives from Grand Compression requires evidence of influence.

Possible evidence may include:

- citation;
- direct acknowledgment;
- documented access;
- correspondence;
- substantially distinctive copied language;
- another demonstrable connection.

Without such evidence:

```text
similarity
≠
derivation
```

Do not infer plagiarism, copying, or unauthorized use merely from conceptual overlap.

---

# P.14 Attribution and Independent Development

If an external researcher independently develops a concept without deriving it from Grand Compression, that independent work does not automatically require attribution to Robbie George merely because the ideas resemble one another.

Required distinction:

```text
independent development
≠
citation obligation created by similarity alone
```

Attribution is appropriate when:

- Grand Compression was actually used;
- its terminology was adopted;
- its framework was cited or implemented;
- its distinctive authored material informed the work.

This protects both Grand Compression authorship and legitimate independent scholarship.

# P.16 Authorship Conservation Rule

The Authorship Conservation Rule preserves the distinction between:

```text
originating framework authorship
```

and:

```text
later implementation, analysis, testing, criticism, or transformation
```

A person or system that:

- implements;
- tests;
- benchmarks;
- summarizes;
- criticizes;
- translates;
- serializes;
- visualizes;

Grand Compression material does not thereby become the author of the originating framework.

Likewise, evidence produced by an external evaluator does not transfer authorship of the claim being tested.

---

# P.17 Evidence Authorship Boundary

Claim provenance and evidence provenance are separate.

Example:


If the independent experiment tests a Grand Compression proposition:

```text
evidence authorship
≠

```

and:

```text
claim authorship
≠
evidence authorship
```

Both should be preserved.

# P.21 Thermodynamic Claims Boundary

Earlier wording described a universal invariant:


Required distinction:


# P.22 Efficiency Boundary

- tokens;
- latency;
- FLOPs;
- model calls;
- joules;
- financial cost;
- memory;
- task utility.

A claim that an architecture is more efficient must identify the relevant metric.

Required distinction:

```text
fewer tokens
≠
lower physical energy automatically
```

and:

```text
greater compression
≠
greater efficiency automatically
```

# P.23 Stability Boundary

Stability also requires an operational definition.

Possible measures include:

- variance;
- failure rate;
- correction demand;
- repeatability;
- state preservation;
- degradation under depth.

Therefore:

```text
structural alignment
≠
stability proven
```

without measurement.

# P.24 “Copycat Immunity” Boundary

Earlier versions of Appendix P used the phrase:

```text
Structural Copycat Immunity
```

That phrase should not be interpreted as a factual guarantee that the framework cannot be reproduced, imitated, reverse-engineered, independently reconstructed, or summarized from public material.

Publicly available information can often be:

- studied;
- implemented;
- compared;
- re-expressed;
- independently reconstructed.

The defensible protection mechanism is:

```text
clear provenance
+
version history
+
attribution rules
+
licensing
+
documented authorship
```

not a claim of technical non-derivability.

# P.27 Falsifiability and Priority

Priority and empirical validity are separate dimensions.

A framework may have documented priority for a proposition that later proves incorrect.

Likewise, a later independent researcher may produce stronger empirical evidence concerning a related proposition.

Required distinction:

```text
earlier
≠
correct
```

and:

```text
later
≠
derivative
```

This separation is essential to credible research governance.

# P.33 Predictive Convergence Claims

Statements that future technology or science will increasingly converge toward Grand Compression are predictions.

They should therefore be treated under:

```text
RC-19 — Predictive Evaluation Requirement
```

A prediction should identify:

- what convergence means;
- observable criteria;
- time horizon;
- comparison set;
- failure condition.

Without these, the statement remains a speculative expectation rather than an empirical finding.
