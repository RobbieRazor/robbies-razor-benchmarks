## Architectural Orientation

Boundary Avoidance describes a failure mode in which a system responds to internal recursion cost or structural inefficiency by expanding external resource, infrastructure, spatial, energetic, computational, or organizational boundaries instead of reducing the underlying internal burden.

Recursive Compression describes the contrasting architectural response in which the system attempts to reduce internal recursion cost while preserving the structure required for continued operation and reuse.

A bounded evaluation should declare:

- the system boundary
- the internal recursion cost
- the external resources being expanded
- the relevant time scale
- the available alternatives
- the observed behavior
- the evidence status
- the failure conditions
- the revision conditions

The existence of external expansion does not by itself prove Boundary Avoidance. The analysis must show how the expansion relates to an identifiable internal recursion cost or structural inefficiency.
