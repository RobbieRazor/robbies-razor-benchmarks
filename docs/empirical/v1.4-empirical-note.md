# 1. Research Question

The experiment examined the bounded hypothesis:

> Under a fixed depth and declared capsule policy, changing source-refresh cadence may affect retention of selected canonical facts across repeated compression steps.

A broader motivating hypothesis was:

> Recursive retention under finite resources may exhibit non-monotonic behavior under some memory–compute or refresh-allocation regimes.

The experiment was capable of producing evidence relevant to that hypothesis.

It was not designed to establish the hypothesis universally.

---

Historical run configuration:

```text

8 steps

gpt-4.1-mini

Temperature setting:
0

8–12 bullets
≤12 words each

Output:
FACT LINES + CAPSULE
```

Three broad regimes were evaluated.

## Compute-heavy

```text
SOURCE available at every step
```

## Memory-heavy

```text

```

```text

```

```text
{1,3,5,7}
```

# 4. Scoring

The historical experiment scored:

```text

```

across 20 canonical fact lines per fixture.

The scoring approach emphasized retention of distinctive items such as:

- identifiers;
- numbers;
- dates;
- thresholds;
- limits;
- constraint terms.

It was designed to reduce sensitivity to ordinary paraphrase.

This does not mean the scoring method has been independently validated as fully paraphrase-robust.

Required distinction:


# 5. What the Score Measures

A score such as:

```text

```

means that 18 of the 20 declared fact signatures were retained under the historical scoring procedure.

It does not directly measure:

- factual reasoning quality generally;
- truthfulness;
- general intelligence;
- semantic coherence;
- physical energy;
- production reliability.

The score is benchmark-local.

# 33. Conservative Interpretation

The current evidence supports the following bounded conclusions:

1. Fact retention varied by regime in both fixtures.
2. Compute-heavy produced the highest retention in both recorded fixtures.
3. The ID-collision fixture showed balanced scores of `16, 16, 18`.
4. The constraint-heavy fixture showed balanced scores of `9, 11, 8`.
5. The constraint-heavy balanced sequence was non-monotonic.
6. Several constraint-like fact types were repeatedly absent in the constraint-heavy run.
7. The experiment does not establish a universal memory–compute optimum.
8. Further replication is required.
