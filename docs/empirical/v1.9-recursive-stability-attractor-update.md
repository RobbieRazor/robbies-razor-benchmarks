## 1. Recursive Stability Attractor

### Historical Framework Orientation

The Recursive Stability Attractor describes a proposed pathway through which recursive systems may approach a Stability Minimum over repeated cycles.

The conceptual sequence is:

```text
expansion
→ constraint accumulation
→ compression innovation
→ stability restoration
→ renewed expansion
```

Within the historical framework, repeated cycles were described as potentially approaching the Stability Minimum defined in §11.4.

This is a framework model.

A specific system should not be described as following this sequence without declared observations, variables, scale, evidence, alternatives, and failure conditions.

## 2. Unified Recursion Efficiency Relation

### Historical Variables

The historical update defined:

- **R** — recursive transition rate
- **E** — available energy per unit time
- **JCT** — Joules per Coherent Transition
- **I** — preserved functional information

The historical recursion-efficiency relation was written as:

```text
Sᵣ = I / JCT
```

The energetic recursion ceiling was written as:

```text
R ≤ E / JCT
```

Substitution produced:

```text
R ≤ (E × Sᵣ) / I
```

### Historical Interpretation

The relation was intended to represent the proposition that recursive throughput may increase when:

- available energy increases; or
- more required functional structure is preserved per declared energetic cost.

These equations are conceptual unless a study defines:

- the system boundary
- recursion transition
- coherence criterion
- information measure
- compatible units
- energy-measurement method
- time interval
- baseline
- uncertainty
- failure conditions

The presence of an equation in a canonical or historical document does not independently establish that it has been empirically validated as a universal physical law.

## 3. Conservation of Compressed Structure

### Historical Variables

The historical update defined:

- **I₍c₎** — compressed structure
- **Cᵣ** — recomputation cost
- **E** — energetic cost

The historical proportionality was written as:

```text
E ∝ Cᵣ
```

The intended framework interpretation was that loss of reusable compressed structure may force additional reconstruction or recomputation.

The historical response alternatives were:

- preserve compressed structure through memory; or
- incur a recomputation burden.

This concept was connected to:

- Compression–Memory Separation — MRD §11.2
- Memory–Compute Trade Curve — MRD §11.4.5A
- Energetic Recursion Ceiling — MRD §11.4.5
