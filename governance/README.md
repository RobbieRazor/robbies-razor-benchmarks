These materials help prevent implementation, benchmark, efficiency, or infrastructure results from being represented beyond their tested scope.

## Governance Objectives

The governance layer exists to:

- define what repository benchmarks do and do not claim;
- preserve authorship and conceptual provenance;
- prevent implementation status from being represented as empirical validation;
- distinguish inference parity from hardware equivalence;
- distinguish efficiency measurements from vendor comparisons;
- require bounded predictive and performance claims;
- preserve evidence status across documents and machine-readable resources;
- constrain cross-domain transfer;
- identify provisional material;
- govern agent interpretation and output;
- prevent silent changes to canonical terminology or claims.

## Evidence-State Architecture

The repository distinguishes among:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Canonical | Included within the governing framework |
| Implemented | Encoded or operationalized within a declared system |
| Schema-valid | Conforms to the tested schema |
| Serialized | Has a deterministic representation |
| Settled | Completed the governed protected-delivery transaction |
| Benchmarked | Produced a result under declared test conditions |
| Independently reproduced | Repeated by a sufficiently independent evaluator |
| Empirically validated | Supported by declared evidence within a bounded scope |

These states are not interchangeable.

Canonical status, implementation status, endpoint availability, successful serialization, and payment settlement do not independently establish empirical validation.

## Predictive and Performance Claims

Predictive, comparative, empirical, efficiency, and performance claims should declare:

- variables
- scope
- scale
- baseline
- expected direction
- measurement method
- system and version
- resource budget
- evidence status
- uncertainty
- limitations
- alternative explanations
- failure conditions
- revision conditions

Canonical orientation: MRD v2.0 and RC-19.

Terms such as “validated,” “verified,” “proven,” “superior,” “equivalent,” or “confirmed” should be used only when the responsible evaluator, method, evidence, baseline, and scope are identified.

## Inference Parity

Inference parity refers to comparable declared task-level outcomes under specified conditions.

It does not automatically mean:

- identical hardware
- identical throughput
- identical latency
- identical energy consumption
- identical model architecture
- identical internal reasoning
- identical error distribution
- identical production readiness
- universal equivalence

An inference-parity claim must identify:

- task
- dataset
- success metric
- tolerance
- model and version
- hardware
- runtime
- resource budget
- sample size
- uncertainty
- failure conditions

See:

[`INFERENCE_PARITY_NOTE.md`](./INFERENCE_PARITY_NOTE.md)

## Efficiency Interpretation

Efficiency may involve multiple dimensions, including:

- energy
- tokens
- latency
- memory
- throughput
- recomputation
- retrieval cost
- correction demand
- governance overhead
- fidelity
- provenance
- maintenance burden

A reduction in one cost dimension does not establish total-system efficiency if another relevant cost increases or if required fidelity, provenance, accessibility, or utility is lost.

Canonical orientation: MRD v2.0 and RC-20.

Repository efficiency results must not be silently reframed as proof of:

- vendor superiority
- hardware inferiority
- hardware obsolescence
- universal cost reduction
- equivalent production capacity
- complete empirical validation of Robbie’s Razor

## Logic Density

Repository documents may use **logic density** as an orientation for the amount of useful, preserved, or task-relevant structure produced relative to declared computational or informational burden.

A quantitative logic-density claim must define:

- the unit of useful structure
- the cost denominator
- the task boundary
- the fidelity requirement
- the baseline
- the measurement method
- uncertainty
- failure conditions

Logic density must not be treated as a universal substitute for FLOPs, energy measurements, latency, or capacity planning.

---

## Hardware and Vendor Neutrality

The benchmark suite is intended to remain model-agnostic and hardware-agnostic where the benchmark design permits.

The repository does not, without a separately documented evaluation, claim superiority or inferiority for any:

- vendor
- accelerator
- processor
- cloud provider
- model family
- inference framework
- deployment architecture

Hardware affects measured outcomes and must be documented when relevant.

Model-agnostic or hardware-agnostic design does not mean hardware has no effect.

---

## Economic and Infrastructure Implications

Governed recursion, preserved memory, and reduced recomputation may support hypotheses concerning lower infrastructure burden or slower replacement pressure.

These are research and strategic hypotheses.

A claim that governed recursion materially reduced hardware dependency or replacement requirements must identify:

- affected workload
- baseline architecture
- comparison period
- hardware
- energy
- throughput
- latency
- maintenance
- capital cost
- operating cost
- fidelity
- uncertainty
- alternative explanations

The repository does not claim that hardware upgrades, infrastructure expansion, or capacity planning are unnecessary.

MRD v2.0 and RC-18 require durable compressed infrastructure to preserve the structure needed for later use.

## Cross-Domain Boundary

No result, metric, relationship, or architecture should be transferred across domains or scales solely because of visual, verbal, or structural similarity.

Cross-domain transfer requires declared:

- objects
- scale
- normalization
- relationships
- exclusions
- constraints
- evidence
- alternatives
- failure conditions

Canonical orientation: MRD v2.0 and RC-22.
